How is this chance calculated?

What xG is: the chance a shot like this one scores, from 0 (never) to 1 (always). It is learned from what really happened to thousands of similar shots.

The model: a LightGBM classifier, which is a set of decision trees that each correct the mistakes of the ones before. It was trained on Premier League shots from , each labelled goal or no goal. It was checked against Understat's own xG, and for calibration: shots it gives a 10% chance should go in about 10% of the time.

What goes into it:

  • Distance to goal, in metres
  • Angle: how much of the goal mouth the shooter can see
  • How far wide of the centre of the pitch the shot was taken
  • Body part: right foot, left foot, header or other
  • Type of chance: open play, corner, set piece, direct free kick or penalty
  • What happened just before: a pass, cross, through ball, dribble, rebound and so on

The formula:

1. Measure where the shot was taken. dx is metres from the goal line, dy is metres off centre, and the goal is 7.32 m wide:

distance = √(dx² + dy²)
angle = arctan( 7.32·dx / (dx² + dy² − 3.66²) )

2. The model's 300 decision trees turn the inputs into a score z. It splits into a starting score z₀ for an average shot plus one push φ per factor:

z = z₀ + φdistance + φangle + φwidth + φbody part + φtype of chance + φprevious action

3. The score becomes a chance between 0 and 1:

xG = 1 / (1 + e−z)

How the list above works: the pushes φ are added to z₀ one at a time, biggest first, and each row shows how much that step moved the chance: 100 × (xG after − xG before) percentage points. Because 1 / (1 + e−z) is a curve, not a straight line, a factor's points depend a little on the order, but the total always lands on the final chance.

What it doesn't know: where defenders and the goalkeeper were, how hard the ball was hit, or who took the shot. Two shots from the same spot in the same way get the same xG.
